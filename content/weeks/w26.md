## D1 · Live coding: how to run the round, and timed PyTorch drills

**Process (interviewers grade this as much as the code):**

1. Restate the problem and confirm input/output shapes and dtypes.
2. Write the function signature and a tiny test first (e.g. compare with `F.scaled_dot_product_attention` or a NumPy loop).
3. Talk through the approach and complexity **before** typing.
4. Implement in small, runnable steps; check shapes with asserts as you go.
5. Handle edge cases (masks, padding, empty input, numerical stability).
6. State complexity and how you'd optimise (vectorise, fuse, batch).

**Timed drills this week** (re-solve from a blank file; don't look at your old solution):

* Multi-head attention with causal + padding masks — 15 min (week 10 exercise).
* RMSNorm, LayerNorm, SwiGLU, RoPE — 15 min total (weeks 6, 10, 11).
* A full tiny GPT forward + greedy/top-p generation with KV cache — 45 min (today's exercise).
* Training loop with AMP, accumulation, clipping, eval — 15 min (week 9).
* MoE layer with top-k routing and aux loss — 25 min (week 12).
* LoRA linear + merge — 10 min (week 17).

Use the Exercises page filter "★ must-know" and the Mock page for spoken parts.

> **Interview angle.** If you get stuck, say what you're trying to do and simplify (single head first, no mask first), then generalise.
> Silence is worse than a partial, well-explained solution.

## D2 · Timed ML-from-scratch in NumPy, and sampling

Re-implement from a blank file with a timer:

* Logistic regression with gradient descent and L2 (10 min), softmax cross-entropy forward/backward (10 min).
* k-means with k-means++ (15 min), PCA via SVD (5 min), GMM EM step (20 min).
* kNN with vectorised distances (10 min), NMS (15 min), ROC-AUC from ranks (10 min).
* **Sampling** (today's exercise): temperature, top-k, top-p (nucleus), repetition penalty, with numerically stable softmax, and correct
  handling of ties and the "at least one token" rule in top-p.

**Common evaluation points:** vectorisation instead of Python loops, numerical stability (log-sum-exp, epsilons), correct axis/keepdims,
random seeds for reproducibility, and clean function boundaries.

> **Interview angle.** For top-p, sort probabilities descending, take the cumulative sum, keep the smallest prefix whose mass ≥ p
> (always including the top token), renormalise and sample — and mention that `torch.multinomial` expects non-negative weights.

## D3 · The debugging round

Many ML interviews now include "here's a training script that doesn't learn — fix it". Today's exercise is a deliberately broken
training function with several planted bugs.

**Method:**

1. Run it; read the error or observe the loss. Check the loss at init (≈ log K for classification).
2. Overfit a single batch. If it can't, the bug is in model/loss/optimiser, not data size.
3. Walk the pipeline: data (shapes, labels aligned, normalisation), model (eval/train modes, masks, activations before the loss),
   loss (logits vs probabilities, reduction, ignore_index), optimiser (zero_grad, parameters passed, LR), scheduler (step frequency),
   evaluation (no_grad, metric computed correctly).
4. Fix one thing at a time and re-run; explain each fix and how you'd prevent it (tests, asserts).

Keep the week 9 bug list handy and add every new bug you meet to it.

> **Interview angle.** Narrate hypotheses ("loss is constant at log 10 → the model's output isn't changing → are gradients reaching the
> weights?") — it shows systematic debugging rather than random edits.

## D4 · Project deep dives and behavioural questions

**Project stories** (prepare 3, each with a 2-minute and a 10-minute version): e.g. your **S-MoE + DCUNet** speech enhancement work, the
**cabin voice lab** (AEC + DNN NS car voice-assistant simulation), and the **KEMAR spatial audio lab** or hearing-aid work. Structure:

* Problem and why it mattered (product/user impact).
* Constraints (latency, compute, data).
* Baseline and what you built; key technical decisions and alternatives you rejected.
* Results with numbers, test conditions and how you validated them.
* What went wrong and what you learned; what you'd do next.
* Your specific contribution (say "I").

Expect deep follow-ups: "why that loss?", "how did you choose the expert count?", "what was the latency on the target?", "what if the
noise type is unseen?".

**Behavioural (STAR: situation, task, action, result):** prepare stories for: a technical disagreement; a failure or bug that reached
users; working with ambiguous requirements; mentoring/helping a colleague; prioritising under a deadline; learning a new area fast (this
6-month plan is a good example — moving from acoustic tuning into ML engineering).

**Questions to ask interviewers:** how models get from research to devices; how they evaluate on-device quality; the hardware targets
and toolchain; what a successful first six months looks like; how ML and DSP/audio teams collaborate.

> **Interview angle.** Your acoustic-tuning and ITU-T measurement background is a differentiator for audio/automotive ML roles —
> frame it as "I know how speech quality is measured and perceived in real cars, and I can build and deploy the models".

## Questions

??* d1 [interview-skills] | How should you approach a live "implement this in PyTorch" interview question?
Clarify inputs/outputs, shapes and dtypes; write the signature and a small test or reference comparison first; explain the approach and complexity
before coding; implement incrementally with shape asserts; handle masks, padding and numerical stability; finish by stating complexity and possible
optimisations. Think aloud throughout, and if stuck, simplify the problem (one head, no mask) and build back up.

??* d2 [decoding] | Implement top-p (nucleus) sampling — describe the algorithm and edge cases.
Apply temperature to logits (divide by T), softmax (stable), sort probabilities descending, cumulative-sum them, and keep the smallest set of top
tokens whose cumulative probability reaches p — always keeping at least the highest-probability token (mask positions where cumsum − prob ≥ p). Zero
out the rest, renormalise, sample with torch.multinomial, and map back to original indices. Edge cases: T → 0 (use greedy), p = 1 (no filtering),
ties, combining with top-k (apply top-k first), batch dimension, and very peaked distributions.

?? d2 [decoding] | What does a repetition penalty do and how is it implemented?
It discourages repeating tokens already in the context: for each previously generated (or present) token, divide its logit by a penalty θ > 1 if the
logit is positive and multiply by θ if negative (so it always decreases), before sampling. Frequency and presence penalties subtract values proportional
to counts. Too strong a penalty harms fluency (e.g. it penalises necessary repeated words or code tokens).

??* d3 [debugging] | A classification model's training loss stays at about 2.30 for 10 classes. What does that tell you and what do you check?
2.30 ≈ ln(10): the model predicts a uniform distribution and isn't learning at all. Check that gradients reach the parameters (optimizer has the model's
parameters, zero_grad/step called, requires_grad not disabled, no detach), the learning rate isn't zero or absurdly small/large, the loss gets logits and
integer labels (no double softmax), labels are aligned with inputs and not constant, inputs aren't all zeros/NaN, and the model can overfit a single batch.

??* d4 [behavioural] | How do you structure a project story for an ML interview?
Problem and impact; constraints; baseline; what you built and key decisions with rejected alternatives; results with numbers, test conditions and
validation; what went wrong and what you learned; your specific contribution; next steps. Have a 2-minute version and a 10-minute version, and be ready
for deep technical follow-ups on any decision.

?? d4 [behavioural] | How would you answer "Why are you moving from acoustic tuning to ML engineering?"
Frame it as a natural progression rather than an escape: tuning cars to ITU-T speech-quality requirements taught you how speech is measured and perceived
in real acoustic environments and where classic DSP hits its limits; you've built ML systems on top of that (DNN noise suppression, AEC simulations, MoE
speech enhancement, spatial audio) and want to own models end to end, from training to on-device deployment. Give a concrete example and connect it to the
role.

??* d5 [interview-skills] | You have one week before an ML engineer interview. How do you prepare?
Find out the loop format and role focus; prioritise high-yield topics for that focus (fundamentals, transformers, the role's specialty such as embedded
optimisation); do daily timed coding of must-know implementations; review high-yield questions out loud with spaced repetition; prepare and rehearse
three project stories and STAR stories; run at least two full mock loops; research the company's products and tech; and rest the day before. (This app's
"Interview in a week" page generates exactly this plan.)
