## D1 · Why DeepSeek matters, and the timeline

DeepSeek (spun out of the quant fund High-Flyer, Hangzhou) became the reference example of frontier-level open models built
with constrained compute. Characteristic traits: **efficiency-first architecture research**, **hardware co-design** around export-
restricted GPUs (H800, reduced interconnect), **detailed technical reports**, **open weights under permissive licences** (MIT),
and open-sourced infrastructure.

**Timeline:**

* **DeepSeek LLM (2023)** — Llama-like dense models; scaling-law studies on data quality and hyper-parameters.
* **DeepSeekMoE (Jan 2024)** — fine-grained + shared experts.
* **DeepSeekMath (Feb 2024)** — math data pipeline + **GRPO**.
* **DeepSeek-V2 (May 2024)** — **MLA** + DeepSeekMoE; 236B total / 21B active; very low API prices.
* **DeepSeek-V3 (Dec 2024)** — 671B / 37B; aux-loss-free balancing, MTP, **FP8 training**, DualPipe; ~2.8M H800 GPU-hours.
* **DeepSeek-R1 (Jan 2025)** — reasoning via large-scale RL; R1-Zero; distilled small models; triggered a market shock.
* **Open Infra week (Feb 2025)** — FlashMLA, DeepEP, DeepGEMM, DualPipe, EPLB, 3FS file system.
* **V3.1 (Aug 2025)** — hybrid thinking/non-thinking in one model, agent/tool use; **V3.2 (Sep–Dec 2025)** — **DSA** sparse attention,
  larger RL compute, agentic data synthesis.
* **mHC paper (Dec 2025).**
* **DeepSeek-V4 (Apr 2026)** — V4-Pro 1.6T/49B and V4-Flash 284B/13B, 1M context, CSA/HCA hybrid attention, mHC, Muon, FP4 experts.

> **Interview angle.** If asked "why was R1 such a big deal?", mention: open weights with near-frontier reasoning, a transparent
> RL recipe, a much lower reported cost, and distilled small models anyone could run — it changed assumptions about who can build
> frontier models.

## D2 · DeepSeek-V2: MLA and DeepSeekMoE in detail

**MLA dimensions (V2/V3):** hidden 5,120 (V2) / 7,168 (V3), 128 heads, per-head dims $d_h^{nope}=128$ + $d_h^{rope}=64$, KV latent
$d_c=512$, query latent $d_c'=1{,}536$ (queries are also low-rank compressed to save activation memory).

* Cache per token per layer: $c^{KV}$ (512) + shared RoPE key $k^R$ (64) = **576 values**.
* Equivalent to GQA with ~2.25 groups in cache size, but with MHA-level (actually slightly better) quality in their ablations.
* V2 reported **93.3% lower KV cache** than DeepSeek 67B (dense MHA) and **5.76× higher maximum generation throughput**.

**Decoupled RoPE:** RoPE on keys would make $k=R_m W^{UK}c$ position-dependent, and $W^{UK}$ could no longer be absorbed into the
query. So keys = [content part from the latent (no RoPE), rotary part $k^R=\mathrm{RoPE}(W^{KR}h)$ shared across heads]; queries
similarly split. Scores = content-content + rope-rope.

**DeepSeekMoE in V2:** 2 shared + 160 routed experts, 6 active; **device-limited routing** (a token's experts on at most M devices)
and three auxiliary losses (expert-level, device-level, communication balance) plus token dropping at the device level — V3 later
replaced the losses with bias-based balancing.

**Why it mattered:** MLA attacked the biggest serving cost (KV cache) and enabled DeepSeek's aggressive pricing; it has since been
adopted or converted to by Kimi K2/K3, GLM-5 and others, and V3/V4 kept it.

> **Interview angle.** Be able to compute MLA's cache (576 × L × bytes) vs MHA (2 × 128 × 128 × L × bytes) — about 57× smaller —
> and explain why RoPE had to be decoupled.

## D3 · DeepSeek-V3: the efficiency stack

**Architecture:** 61 layers (first 3 dense), MLA, MoE with 1 shared + 256 routed experts, top-8, sigmoid affinities, **aux-loss-free
balancing** (week 12) + tiny sequence-wise loss, node-limited routing (≤ 4 nodes), no token dropping.

**Multi-token prediction (MTP):** $D=1$ extra sequential module: for position $i$, combine the main model's representation with the
embedding of token $i+1$ (RMSNorm both, concatenate, project), pass through one transformer block, predict token $i+2$ with the
**shared** output head. Loss weight λ (0.3 then 0.1). Benefits: denser training signal, encourages planning; at inference, used as a
speculative draft (~85–90% acceptance) or discarded.

**FP8 mixed precision:**

* GEMMs (forward, activation-grad and weight-grad) in **FP8 E4M3** — the same format for all, thanks to fine-grained scaling.
* **Fine-grained quantisation:** activations scaled per **1×128 tile** (per token per 128 channels), weights per **128×128 block** —
  outliers only affect their own tile.
* **Increased accumulation precision:** Hopper Tensor Cores accumulate FP8 products with limited precision (~14 bits), so partial
  sums are promoted to FP32 on CUDA cores every 128 elements.
* Master weights, optimiser states (BF16 moments), embeddings, output head, MoE gating, norms and attention kept in higher precision.
* Loss within 0.25% relative error of BF16 baseline.

**Parallelism:** DualPipe (bidirectional pipeline with compute/communication overlap), 64-way EP, ZeRO-1, no TP; custom all-to-all
kernels using ~20 SMs.

**Training:** 14.8T tokens; context extended to 32K then 128K with **YaRN**; post-training with SFT (including reasoning data
**distilled from R1**) and RL (GRPO). Total 2.788M H800 GPU-hours.

> **Interview angle.** "Why fine-grained FP8 scaling?" — LLM activations have outlier channels; one per-tensor scale would make the
> 3-bit mantissa useless for everything else. Per-tile scales confine outliers' damage and make FP8 E4M3 usable even for gradients.

## D4 · DeepSeek-R1: reasoning through RL, and distillation

**R1-Zero:** apply GRPO **directly to the base model** (V3-Base) with only rule-based rewards — **accuracy** (math answer matches,
code passes tests) and **format** (reasoning in `<think>` tags). No SFT, no neural reward model (avoids reward hacking). Over training,
response length grows and behaviours like self-verification, reflection and backtracking emerge (the "aha moment"); AIME accuracy
rose from ~16% to ~71% (pass@1). Problems: poor readability, language mixing.

**R1 pipeline (4 stages):**

1. **Cold start:** SFT on thousands of curated long-CoT examples (readable format).
2. **Reasoning-oriented RL** (GRPO) with accuracy + a **language-consistency** reward.
3. **Rejection sampling + SFT:** sample ~600K reasoning traces from the RL checkpoint (keep correct, readable ones) + ~200K
   non-reasoning samples (writing, QA, from V3's pipeline) → SFT the base model again.
4. **RL for all scenarios:** rule rewards for reasoning; reward models for helpfulness/harmlessness.

**Distillation:** SFT Qwen2.5 and Llama 3 models (1.5B–70B) on the 800K samples → R1-Distill models; the 7B/14B/32B beat much larger
non-reasoning models on math. Key finding: **distilling from a strong reasoner beats running RL on the small model directly** —
small models lack the capacity to discover the behaviours through RL alone. This is the most relevant lesson for edge: get
reasoning into small models by distillation.

**Unsuccessful attempts they reported:** process reward models (hard to define steps, reward hacking) and MCTS (token-level search
space too large) — honest negative results that interviewers like you to know.

> **Interview angle.** "What made R1-Zero work?" — a strong base model, verifiable rewards that can't be gamed easily, GRPO's
> cheap advantage estimation, and enough RL steps for long reasoning to emerge; the KL to the reference and format reward keep it on
> track.

## D5 · V3.2 and V4: sparse attention, mHC, Muon, FP4 — and the open-infra legacy

**V3.2 / DSA:** continued training of V3.1 with **DeepSeek Sparse Attention** — a lightning indexer selects the top-2,048 tokens
per query for MLA attention (two-stage training: indexer warm-up distilled from dense attention, then sparse training). Long-context
cost drops sharply with little quality loss; prices were cut accordingly. V3.2 also scaled RL compute and used large-scale
synthesised agentic tasks (environments, tools) for post-training.

**V4 (preview, Apr 2026):**

* **Hybrid CSA + HCA attention** (week 13): 4× compressed + top-k sparse, alternated with 128× heavily compressed dense attention,
  both with sliding windows; V4-Pro's 61 layers start with HCA then alternate. FP8 KV (RoPE dims BF16), FP4 indexer.
  At 1M context: ~27% of V3.2's per-token FLOPs, ~10% of its KV cache (Flash: 10% / 7%).
* **mHC** with 4 residual streams (Sinkhorn-projected, doubly stochastic mixing), replacing plain residuals.
* **Muon** for most parameters (AdamW for embeddings, head, norms, mHC static parameters/gates); they report not needing QK-clip.
* **FP4 quantisation-aware training** of MoE expert weights (instruct checkpoints: FP4 experts, FP8 elsewhere) — halves expert memory
  vs FP8 for serving, with FP4 integrated into the RL/rollout infrastructure too.
* **MoE:** DeepSeekMoE with hash routing in the first layers; V4-Flash: 256 experts, 6 routed + 1 shared per token.
* **Training:** ~32–33T tokens with staged extension to 1M; **post-training:** domain specialists trained with SFT + GRPO
  (verifiers and generative rewards), merged via **on-policy distillation**; three reasoning modes (non-think, think, max).
* **Infrastructure:** TileLang kernels, fine-grained EP communication/compute overlap, batch-invariant deterministic kernels (for
  consistent RL between inference and training), memory-efficient mHC implementation.
* Positioning: very cheap per token (V4-Flash API ≈ $0.14/M input), competitive with but not uniformly ahead of the best closed
  models.

**Open-sourced infrastructure** worth naming: FlashMLA (MLA decode kernel), DeepEP (MoE all-to-all), DeepGEMM (FP8 GEMM with
fine-grained scaling), DualPipe, EPLB (expert load balancer), 3FS (distributed file system), and the ISCA 2025 paper on hardware
lessons (e.g. calls for better FP8 accumulation, unified scale-up/scale-out networking, and native fine-grained quantisation support —
which newer formats like MXFP8/MXFP4 address).

> **Interview angle.** "What's the throughline of DeepSeek's architecture work?" — relentlessly cutting the bytes that dominate cost:
> KV cache (MLA → DSA → CSA/HCA + FP8 KV), weights and activations (FP8 → FP4), communication (node-limited routing, DualPipe, DeepEP),
> and the critic in RL (GRPO).

## Questions

??* d2 [mla, chinese-models] | Why did DeepSeek need "decoupled RoPE" in MLA?
MLA's efficiency relies on absorbing the key up-projection W_UK into the query (qᵀW_UK c), so attention runs on the cached latent c. If
RoPE were applied to keys reconstructed from c, the rotation R_m would sit between W_UK and c and depend on the key's position, so W_UK
could no longer be pre-multiplied into the query. DeepSeek therefore splits each head into a non-rotary part computed from the latent and
a small rotary part (64 dims) from a separate projection, with the rotary key shared across heads and cached alongside the latent.

?? d2 [mla, chinese-models] | Quantify MLA's KV-cache saving in DeepSeek-V2/V3.
Per token per layer MLA caches the 512-dim latent plus a 64-dim RoPE key: 576 values. Standard MHA with 128 heads × 128 dims caches K and V:
2 × 16,384 = 32,768 values, ≈57× more. V2 reported a 93.3% KV-cache reduction versus DeepSeek 67B and 5.76× higher maximum generation
throughput.

??* d3 [mtp, chinese-models] | How does DeepSeek-V3's multi-token prediction work and what are its benefits?
After the main model computes the hidden state for position i, an MTP module combines it (RMS-normalised) with the embedding of token
i+1, projects and passes it through one extra transformer block, and predicts token i+2 with the shared output head; modules are
sequential, preserving the causal chain. The MTP loss (weighted ~0.1–0.3) is added to the main loss. Benefits: denser training signal
and better data efficiency, encouraging representations that plan ahead; at inference it can be dropped or used as a speculative-decoding
draft with ~85–90% acceptance (≈1.8× speed).

??* d3 [fp8, chinese-models] | What made FP8 training work for DeepSeek-V3?
Fine-grained scaling (1×128 tiles for activations, 128×128 blocks for weights) to contain outliers so E4M3 can be used everywhere; promoting
Tensor Core partial sums to FP32 every 128 elements to fix limited accumulation precision; keeping sensitive components (embeddings,
output head, gating, norms, attention, master weights and optimiser state) in higher precision; and FP8 storage of cached activations
and communication where safe. Result: near-BF16 accuracy with much lower memory and faster GEMMs.

??* d4 [r1, rl] | What is DeepSeek-R1-Zero and what did it demonstrate?
R1-Zero is DeepSeek-V3-Base trained directly with GRPO using only rule-based accuracy and format rewards — no supervised fine-tuning. It
showed that long chain-of-thought reasoning, self-verification and reflection can emerge from pure RL on a strong base model, with response
length and accuracy growing steadily (AIME pass@1 from ~16% to ~71%). Its outputs had poor readability and language mixing, which the full
R1 pipeline fixed with cold-start SFT and extra stages.

??* d4 [distillation, edge] | What did DeepSeek find about distillation vs RL for small models, and why does it matter for edge AI?
SFT of small Qwen/Llama models on ~800K samples generated by R1 produced much stronger reasoners than applying large-scale RL to the small
models directly; small models lack the capacity/prior to discover long reasoning behaviours through RL alone. For edge deployment this
means the practical recipe is: train or obtain a large teacher, distil its behaviour into a small model (SFT or on-policy distillation),
then quantise — rather than trying to RL-train the small model from scratch.

?? d4 [r1] | What approaches did DeepSeek report as unsuccessful for R1, and why?
Process reward models: hard to define fine-grained steps for general reasoning, hard to label correctness of intermediate steps, and prone
to reward hacking, with extra training cost. Monte Carlo tree search: the token-level search space is enormous and training a fine-grained
value model to guide search is difficult, so it didn't scale as in games. These negative results pushed them toward simple outcome rewards
with RL.

??* d5 [chinese-models] | Summarise the main innovations of DeepSeek-V4.
Hybrid attention alternating CSA (4× KV compression + lightning-indexer top-k sparse selection + sliding window) and HCA (128× compression,
dense) for million-token context, with FP8 KV storage; manifold-constrained hyper-connections (4-stream residuals with doubly stochastic
mixing) for stability; the Muon optimizer for most parameters; FP4 quantisation-aware training for MoE experts; hash routing in early MoE
layers; ~32T-token pre-training with staged extension to 1M; and post-training via specialist SFT+GRPO models merged by on-policy
distillation. At 1M context V4-Pro needs ~27% of V3.2's FLOPs and ~10% of its KV cache.

?? d5 [chinese-models] | Name four pieces of infrastructure DeepSeek open-sourced and what they do.
FlashMLA: optimised MLA decoding kernel for paged KV caches. DeepEP: expert-parallel all-to-all dispatch/combine kernels over NVLink and RDMA
with FP8 support and low-latency modes. DeepGEMM: FP8 GEMM library with fine-grained scaling, including grouped GEMMs for MoE. DualPipe:
bidirectional pipeline-parallel schedule overlapping compute and communication. Also EPLB (expert-parallel load balancer replicating hot
experts) and 3FS (high-throughput distributed file system for training data and KV-cache storage).
