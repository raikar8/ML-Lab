## D1 · Qwen (Alibaba): the most complete open model family

Qwen's strength is **breadth**: dense and MoE models from <1B to ~400B, coder/math/VL/omni/audio variants, Apache-2.0 licences,
and strong small models — which makes Qwen the default base for on-device and research work.

* **Qwen2.5 (2024):** 18T tokens, strong data pipeline; Qwen2.5-Coder/Math; widely used as the base for distillation (R1-Distill-Qwen).
* **Qwen3 (Apr 2025):** dense 0.6B–32B and MoE 30B-A3B / 235B-A22B; **hybrid thinking** — one model with thinking and non-thinking modes
  switchable per request, plus a **thinking budget**; ~36T tokens, 119 languages; **QK-norm**, no QKV bias; small models trained by
  **strong-to-weak distillation** from the flagship (logit distillation off- and on-policy), much cheaper than RL for each size.
  Later 2507 updates split instruct and thinking models. **GSPO** for RL (sequence-level ratios — week 17).
* **Qwen3-Next (Sep 2025, 80B-A3B):** the architecture prototype — **3:1 Gated DeltaNet : gated attention** hybrid, ultra-sparse MoE
  (512 experts, 10 routed + 1 shared), output-gated attention (removes sinks/massive activations), zero-centred and weight-decayed
  layer-norm weights for stability, MTP for speculative decoding; ~10% of Qwen3-32B's training cost, >10× throughput beyond 32K context.
* **Qwen3.5 (Feb 2026):** the hybrid goes mainstream — 397B-A17B flagship (60 layers: 15 × [3 DeltaNet + 1 gated attention], 262K
  native, ~1M with YaRN), **natively multimodal** (trained on text, images and video together), MTP; medium (27B, 35B-A3B, 122B-A10B)
  and small (0.8B–9B) models also use the 3:1 DeltaNet hybrid — so **on-device models now have linear-attention layers** (Qwen3.5-0.8B
  runs in LiteRT-LM, ExecuTorch and llama.cpp builds). Qwen3.6 continues the recipe.

> **Interview angle.** "Why are Qwen models popular as base models?" — permissive licence, every size, strong small models via
> distillation, good multilingual/code ability, and broad tooling support (vLLM, llama.cpp, MLX, ExecuTorch, LiteRT).

## D2 · Kimi (Moonshot AI): optimisers, linear attention and scale

* **Kimi k1.5 (Jan 2025):** RL scaling with very long contexts and **partial rollouts** (continue long trajectories across iterations),
  long-to-short methods to make reasoning concise.
* **Muon at scale ("Muon is Scalable", Moonlight 16B-A3B):** added weight decay and per-matrix update RMS matching to make Muon work for
  LLMs; ~2× compute efficiency vs AdamW in their study.
* **Kimi K2 (Jul 2025):** 1T total / 32B active MoE, 384 experts (8 + 1 shared), MLA, **fewer attention heads (64)** than DeepSeek-V3
  (128) to cut long-context inference cost; **MuonClip** (Muon + QK-clip) → 15.5T tokens with no loss spikes; large-scale **agentic data
  synthesis** (simulated tools and environments) and RL with verifiable rewards plus **self-critique rubric rewards** for open-ended tasks.
* **Kimi K2 Thinking (Nov 2025):** long-horizon tool-using reasoning (hundreds of sequential tool calls) and **native INT4 weights via
  quantisation-aware training** in post-training — roughly 2× faster generation at little quality cost; a strong example of QAT used by a
  frontier lab.
* **Kimi Linear (Oct 2025):** **Kimi Delta Attention** (channel-wise gated delta rule) in a 3:1 KDA : MLA hybrid, 48B-A3B.
* **Kimi K2.5 (Jan–Feb 2026):** native multimodal continual pre-training (~15T mixed tokens, MoonViT-3D), **Agent Swarm** (parallel
  sub-agents trained with RL to decompose tasks; up to ~4.5× lower latency than a single agent).
* **Attention Residuals (Mar 2026)** and **Kimi K3 (Jul 2026):** 2.8T total parameters, 93 layers = 69 KDA + 24 gated-MLA layers,
  **896 experts with 16 routed + 2 shared** ("Stable LatentMoE"), AttnRes, native vision, 1M context, released with **MXFP4 weights /
  MXFP8 activations trained with QAT** — the largest open-weight model at release.
* **Mooncake:** Kimi's KV-cache-centric disaggregated serving system (week 14).

> **Interview angle.** Kimi's throughline: optimiser and architecture research aimed at token efficiency (Muon, KDA, AttnRes),
> extreme sparsity, and agentic post-training — plus shipping QAT low-bit weights natively.

## D3 · GLM (Zhipu / Z.ai), MiniMax and others

**GLM (Zhipu AI / Z.ai, Tsinghua lineage):**

* **GLM-4.5 (Jul 2025):** 355B-A32B MoE (and 106B-A12B Air), unified "ARC" (agentic, reasoning, coding) model with hybrid thinking;
  design choice of **more layers with smaller width** (deeper rather than wider); **slime** open-source RL framework for agentic RL.
* **GLM-5 (Feb 2026):** adopts **DeepSeek-style MLA + DSA** (256 experts, 8 active, 78 layers, ~200K context), 27T-token pre-training
  prioritising code and reasoning, **asynchronous RL infrastructure** for long agentic tasks; at release the top open-weights model on the
  Artificial Analysis index. GLM-5.1/5.2 refine it; GLM-5.2 adds **IndexShare** (sharing DSA top-k selections across layers).
  GLM shows how fast techniques diffuse between Chinese labs (MLA/DSA from DeepSeek, open papers and code).

**MiniMax:**

* **MiniMax-01 (Jan 2025):** 456B-A46B with **Lightning Attention** hybrid (7 linear : 1 softmax), 1M–4M token context.
* **MiniMax-M1 (Jun 2025):** reasoning model on that hybrid; **CISPO** RL algorithm; full RL in 3 weeks on 512 H800s (~$0.53M rental).
* **MiniMax-M2 (Oct 2025) → M2.5 / M2.7 (2026):** 230B-A10B, **full attention in every layer** (GQA, QK-norm, MTP) after finding hybrid
  attention lagged on complex multi-hop/agentic tasks at scale; **interleaved thinking** (reasoning between tool calls) and RL on
  executable software-engineering environments that accounts for tool latency.

**Other labs worth naming** (one line each): **ByteDance Seed** — hyper-connections (the basis of mHC), DAPO (with Tsinghua), Seed
model series, UltraMem; **StepFun** Step-3 — attention–FFN disaggregation for decoding cost; **Baidu ERNIE 4.5** — heterogeneous
multimodal MoE; **Meituan LongCat-Flash** — "zero-computation" experts so tokens can use a variable amount of compute; **Tencent
Hunyuan**, **Ant Ling**, **Xiaomi MiMo** (small reasoning models).

> **Interview angle.** MiniMax's M1 → M2 reversal is a great story for "how do you decide on an architecture change?": run
> evaluations that reflect the real workload, not proxy benchmarks.

## D4 · The playbook: how did Chinese open models catch up — and where do they stand?

A balanced, evidence-based answer (interviewers notice hype in either direction):

**Techniques they led or popularised in the open:**

1. **Sparse MoE at extreme sparsity** — fine-grained + shared experts, aux-loss-free balancing, 3–5% active parameters, now 16-of-896
   experts (Kimi K3).
2. **Attention redesign for memory** — MLA, DSA, CSA/HCA, Gated DeltaNet / KDA / Lightning hybrids, gated attention.
3. **Low-precision training and serving** — FP8 training at scale (DeepSeek-V3), FP4 QAT experts (V4), INT4 QAT (K2 Thinking),
   MXFP4/MXFP8 (K3).
4. **Optimisers and stability** — Muon/MuonClip, QK-clip, mHC, AttnRes.
5. **Cheap, scalable RL** — GRPO and its family (GSPO, CISPO, DAPO), verifiable rewards, agentic environment synthesis, async RL infra.
6. **Distillation** — R1-Distill, Qwen3 strong-to-weak, on-policy distillation in V4: frontier behaviour in small models.
7. **Systems co-design** — DualPipe, DeepEP, node-limited routing, Mooncake; designing *for* the hardware they could get.

**Why it happened:** export controls made compute scarce, so efficiency was a necessity, not an optimisation; strong engineering talent
and fast iteration; **open weights and detailed reports**, which let labs build on each other (GLM adopting MLA/DSA within months) and
earned global developer adoption; aggressive pricing (DeepSeek-V4-Flash at about $0.14/M input tokens) changing the economics of API
use.

**Where they stand (as of late 2026):** open Chinese models are at or near the frontier on many coding, math and agentic benchmarks,
and lead open-weight rankings; but closed frontier models still lead on several of the hardest evaluations (e.g. Kimi K3 trails Claude
Fable 5 on HLE-Full per Moonshot's own release comparisons; Hugging Face described V4's benchmarks as competitive but not SOTA).
Benchmarks can be saturated or contaminated, so real-world evaluation matters. A good interview answer credits the specific innovations,
notes the constraints that drove them, and avoids sweeping claims.

> **Interview angle.** Structure the answer as: constraints → technical responses (3–4 named techniques) → ecosystem effects (open
> weights, pricing, diffusion of ideas) → honest caveats.

## D5 · What transfers to small and on-device models — and how to read a tech report

**Transferable lessons for embedded/edge AI:**

* **Distil, don't RL, small models** (R1-Distill, Qwen3 small models). On-policy distillation from a big teacher is the strongest recipe.
* **Quantisation-aware training for low-bit weights** (K2 Thinking INT4, V4 FP4 experts, K3 MXFP4): if you control training, QAT beats
  PTQ at 4 bits and below.
* **Hybrid linear attention** for long context on device: constant-size state per DeltaNet layer (Qwen3.5 small models).
* **MTP / draft heads** for speculative decoding on bandwidth-bound devices.
* **Small-active MoE** (A3B) for laptops and high-RAM devices — not for phones/MCUs.
* **Gated attention / QK-norm** reduce activation outliers → friendlier INT8/INT4 quantisation.
* **Micro-scaled formats** (MXFP4/MXFP8, NVFP4) are arriving in NPUs and GPUs — understand block scaling (your exercise this week).

**How to read a tech report in 30 minutes** (and talk about it in an interview): (1) the model card table — total/active params, layers,
attention type, experts, context, precision; (2) architecture diff vs the previous version — what changed and the claimed reason;
(3) training — tokens, data mix, stages, optimiser, precision, parallelism; (4) post-training — SFT data, RL algorithm and rewards,
distillation; (5) efficiency claims — FLOPs, KV cache, throughput, cost, with their baseline; (6) evaluation — which benchmarks, against
whom, with what settings; (7) negative results and limitations. Then write a five-line summary in your own words.

> **Interview angle.** "Tell me about a recent paper you read" is a standard question. Prepare two: one architecture (e.g.
> DeepSeek-V4 or Qwen3.5) and one efficiency/edge paper (e.g. a QAT or on-device inference paper), using the seven-point structure.

## Questions

??* d1 [qwen, chinese-models] | What are Qwen3's hybrid thinking modes and how were its small models trained?
A single Qwen3 model supports a thinking mode (generates an explicit reasoning trace before answering) and a non-thinking mode (answers
directly), selectable per request, with an optional thinking budget that caps reasoning tokens — trading latency for accuracy without
deploying two models. The flagship models went through the full post-training pipeline (long-CoT cold start, reasoning RL, thinking-mode
fusion, general RL), while smaller models were trained by strong-to-weak distillation from them (off-policy then on-policy logit
distillation), which was much cheaper than running RL for each size and produced better small models.

??* d1 [qwen, architecture] | What did Qwen3-Next and Qwen3.5 change architecturally?
They replaced most full-attention layers with Gated DeltaNet linear-attention layers in a 3:1 ratio, keeping one gated full-attention layer
(GQA, partial RoPE, sigmoid output gate) in every four for precise retrieval; used ultra-sparse MoE (e.g. 512 experts with ~10 active in
Qwen3-Next); added output gating to remove attention sinks and massive activations; stabilised training with zero-centred, weight-decayed
norm weights; and added MTP for speculative decoding. Qwen3.5 brought this hybrid to the whole family, including small on-device models,
and made it natively multimodal.

??* d2 [kimi, chinese-models] | What is MuonClip and what problem did it solve for Kimi K2?
Muon trained more token-efficiently than AdamW but, at K2's scale, attention logits grew without bound, causing loss spikes. MuonClip adds
QK-clip: after each step, for heads whose maximum attention logit exceeds a threshold τ, rescale that head's query and key projection weights
by a factor so the max logit returns to τ (split between W_q and W_k). This directly bounds logits at the weight level without changing the
optimiser's behaviour elsewhere, and K2 pre-trained on 15.5T tokens with no loss spikes.

?? d2 [kimi, quantization] | What did Kimi K2 Thinking and Kimi K3 do with quantisation?
K2 Thinking applied quantisation-aware training during post-training to produce native INT4 (weight-only) MoE weights, so the released model
runs at INT4 with little quality loss and roughly double the generation speed — important for long reasoning traces. K3 was released with
MXFP4 weights and MXFP8 activations, also trained with QAT. Both show frontier labs treating low-bit deployment as part of training rather
than an afterthought.

??* d2 [kimi, architecture] | Describe Kimi K3's architecture in one minute.
A 2.8T-parameter MoE with ~104B activated parameters per token, 93 layers: 69 Kimi Delta Attention (channel-wise gated delta-rule linear
attention) layers and 24 gated MLA layers; 896 experts with 16 routed + 2 shared per token in a "Stable LatentMoE" (experts in a reduced latent
dimension); Attention Residuals for depth-wise aggregation; native vision encoder; 1M-token context; MXFP4 weights / MXFP8 activations with
QAT. It combines Moonshot's research threads — KDA, AttnRes, extreme sparsity, low-bit QAT — at the largest open-weight scale to date.

??* d3 [glm, minimax] | How do GLM-5 and MiniMax-M2 differ in their attention choices, and what does that tell you?
GLM-5 adopted DeepSeek-style MLA plus DSA sparse attention to cut long-context training and inference cost. MiniMax-M2 deliberately used full
softmax attention in every layer after its previous models (MiniMax-01/M1) had used a Lightning-attention hybrid, because hybrids underperformed
on complex multi-hop and agentic tasks at scale and efficient-attention infrastructure was less mature. It shows there's no consensus: the right
choice depends on workload, evaluation and infrastructure, and labs validate on their target tasks.

?? d3 [minimax, rl] | What is CISPO?
Clipped IS-weight Policy Optimization, MiniMax-M1's RL algorithm. PPO/GRPO clip the token-level update, which zeroes the gradient for tokens whose
ratio leaves the clip range — often rare, low-probability tokens like "wait" or "however" that trigger reflection. CISPO instead clips the
importance-sampling weight (with stop-gradient) and keeps every token's policy-gradient term, so these tokens still contribute while variance
stays bounded. MiniMax reported faster, more stable RL than DAPO.

??* d4 [chinese-models] | How did Chinese open models catch up with frontier labs? Give a balanced answer.
Constraints: export controls limited access to top GPUs, so labs optimised for efficiency. Technical responses: very sparse MoE (DeepSeekMoE,
aux-loss-free balancing), KV-efficient attention (MLA, DSA, linear-attention hybrids), low-precision training (FP8, FP4/INT4 QAT), better optimisers
(Muon), cheap RL (GRPO family with verifiable rewards), distillation into small models, and communication-aware systems (DualPipe, DeepEP). Ecosystem:
open weights and detailed reports let labs build on each other quickly and won developer adoption; low prices changed API economics. Caveats:
closed frontier models still lead on several of the hardest benchmarks, benchmark gains don't always translate to real-world performance, and
reported training costs usually exclude research and ablations.

?? d4 [chinese-models] | Which DeepSeek ideas were adopted by other labs within a year?
MLA (Kimi K2/K3, GLM-5; conversions like TransMLA), DeepSeekMoE-style fine-grained and shared experts (Qwen, Kimi, GLM, many others), aux-loss-free
or bias-based balancing, GRPO and its variants (nearly every open reasoning pipeline), MTP heads (Qwen3-Next/3.5, MiniMax M2, GLM), DSA sparse attention
(GLM-5), and FP8 training recipes. Hyper-connections came from ByteDance and were constrained by DeepSeek (mHC).

??* d5 [edge, chinese-models] | Which techniques from frontier Chinese models would you apply to an on-device assistant, and which wouldn't you?
Apply: distillation from a large reasoning model into a ~1–4B model (R1-Distill, Qwen3 strong-to-weak), QAT for 4-bit weights, hybrid Gated-DeltaNet
attention for long context with bounded memory, MTP/draft heads for speculative decoding, QK-norm/gated attention for quantisation-friendly
activations, and micro-scaled formats if the NPU supports them. Mostly skip: huge-sparsity MoE (memory = total params), DSA/CSA-style sparse
attention (gains appear at 100K+ contexts, kernels unsupported on NPUs), and training-scale systems tricks (DualPipe, EP).

?? d5 [research-skills] | How do you quickly read and summarise an LLM technical report?
Start with the model card/config (sizes, layers, attention, experts, context, precision); identify the architecture diff from the previous version and
the stated reason; note training data size/mix, stages, optimiser, precision and parallelism; post-training recipe (SFT data, RL algorithm, rewards,
distillation); efficiency claims with their baselines; evaluations and their settings; and limitations/negative results. Then write a five-line summary
and one question you'd ask the authors — that's also how to answer "tell me about a paper you read".
