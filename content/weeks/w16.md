## D1 · Autoencoders, VAEs and VQ-VAEs

An **autoencoder** compresses $x\to z\to\hat x$; a bottleneck forces it to learn structure. Denoising autoencoders reconstruct
clean from corrupted inputs (your enhancement models are conditional denoisers).

A **VAE** makes it generative: encoder $q_\phi(z\mid x)=\mathcal N(\mu,\mathrm{diag}\,\sigma^2)$, prior $p(z)=\mathcal N(0,I)$,
decoder $p_\theta(x\mid z)$. Maximise the **ELBO**:

```math
\log p(x)\ \ge\ \mathbb E_{q_\phi(z\mid x)}[\log p_\theta(x\mid z)]-\mathrm{KL}\big(q_\phi(z\mid x)\,\|\,p(z)\big)
```

with the closed-form Gaussian KL $\tfrac12\sum(\mu^2+\sigma^2-\log\sigma^2-1)$. The **reparameterisation trick**
$z=\mu+\sigma\odot\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ moves the randomness outside the network so gradients flow
through $\mu,\sigma$. Predict $\log\sigma^2$ for stability.

**Issues:** blurry samples (Gaussian likelihood ≈ MSE); **posterior collapse** with powerful decoders (KL → 0, $z$ ignored) —
fixes: KL annealing, free bits; β-VAE trades reconstruction for disentanglement.

**VQ-VAE:** discrete latents from a learned codebook; encoder output snapped to the nearest code; gradients copied through with
the **straight-through estimator**; loss = reconstruction + codebook loss $\|\mathrm{sg}[z_e]-e\|^2$ + commitment loss
$\beta\|z_e-\mathrm{sg}[e]\|^2$ (codebooks often updated by EMA k-means instead). Basis of image tokenizers and, with residual
VQ, neural audio codecs.

**Latent diffusion** (Stable Diffusion) runs diffusion in a VAE's latent space — 8× smaller per side.

> **Interview angle.** Explain why you can't backprop through sampling $z\sim\mathcal N(\mu,\sigma^2)$ directly and how the
> reparameterisation fixes it; contrast with REINFORCE (score-function estimator), which you'll need for RL in week 17.

## D2 · GANs, normalising flows and vocoders

**GANs:** generator $G$ vs discriminator $D$, $\min_G\max_D\ \mathbb E[\log D(x)]+\mathbb E[\log(1-D(G(z)))]$. Sharp samples, fast
one-step generation, but unstable training: **mode collapse**, vanishing generator gradients (use the non-saturating loss),
oscillation. Fixes: Wasserstein loss with gradient penalty, spectral normalisation, two-time-scale LRs, feature matching.
Evaluation: FID.

**Where GANs still win: audio vocoders.** HiFi-GAN, BigVGAN, and the adversarial losses inside neural codecs (EnCodec, DAC)
use multi-period and multi-scale discriminators plus mel-spectrogram and feature-matching losses — fast, high-quality waveform
generation suitable for on-device TTS. MetricGAN uses a discriminator that predicts PESQ to optimise enhancement toward a
perceptual metric.

**Normalising flows:** invertible transforms with tractable Jacobians (coupling layers: RealNVP, Glow) give exact likelihood
$\log p(x)=\log p(z)+\log|\det\partial z/\partial x|$; WaveGlow was a flow vocoder. Architecture constraints limit them; flow
**matching** (D4) borrows the continuous-flow idea without those constraints.

**Autoregressive models** (PixelCNN, WaveNet, LLMs) give exact likelihood and great quality but sequential sampling.

> **Interview angle.** "GAN vs diffusion?" — GANs: one step, sharp, unstable, mode collapse. Diffusion: stable maximum-likelihood
> style training, excellent coverage/quality, slow sampling (mitigated by distillation, flow matching, consistency models).

## D3 · Diffusion models (DDPM, DDIM, guidance)

**Forward process:** gradually add Gaussian noise. With $\alpha_t=1-\beta_t$ and $\bar\alpha_t=\prod_{s\le t}\alpha_s$, any step
can be sampled directly:

```math
q(x_t\mid x_0)=\mathcal N\!\big(\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0,\ (1-\bar\alpha_t)I\big)\ \Longleftrightarrow\ x_t=\sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0+\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\varepsilon
```

**Training (DDPM, Ho et al.):** sample $t$, noise $\varepsilon$, form $x_t$, and regress the noise:
$\mathcal L=\mathbb E\|\varepsilon-\varepsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2$ — a reweighted ELBO, and equivalent to **denoising score
matching**: $\varepsilon_\theta\approx-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\nabla_x\log q(x_t)$ (Tweedie's formula connects noise prediction,
the score and the posterior mean $\mathbb E[x_0\mid x_t]$).

**Sampling (ancestral):** from $x_T\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$,
$x_{t-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\alpha_t}}\big(x_t-\frac{\beta_t}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}}\varepsilon_\theta\big)+\sigma_tz$ — typically
1000 steps. **DDIM** gives a deterministic, non-Markovian sampler with the same training, allowing 20–50 steps; ODE solvers
(DPM-Solver) go to ~10–20.

**Schedules:** linear, cosine (keeps more signal at the start), and SNR-based views; parameterisations: $\varepsilon$, $x_0$, or
**v-prediction** $v=\sqrt{\bar\alpha}\varepsilon-\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha}x_0$ (better at high noise / for distillation).

**Conditioning and guidance:** class/text/audio conditioning via cross-attention or concatenation. **Classifier-free guidance**:
train with the condition dropped sometimes, then sample with $\tilde\varepsilon=(1+w)\varepsilon_\theta(x,c)-w\,\varepsilon_\theta
(x,\varnothing)$ — trades diversity for fidelity.

**Backbones:** U-Nets with attention, and **DiT** (diffusion transformers on patches — Stable Diffusion 3, Sora-style video
models, many audio models).

> **Interview angle.** Write the training loop in 8 lines: sample $t$, $\varepsilon$; $x_t$ from the closed form; loss = MSE
> between $\varepsilon$ and the network output; step. Interviewers check that you know you don't simulate the chain in training.

## D4 · Flow matching, fast sampling and diffusion for speech

**Flow matching / rectified flow:** learn a velocity field $v_\theta(x,t)$ that transports noise $x_0\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$ to data
$x_1$ along straight lines $x_t=(1-t)x_0+tx_1$; the regression target is simply $x_1-x_0$:

```math
\mathcal L=\mathbb E_{t,x_0,x_1}\big\|v_\theta(x_t,t)-(x_1-x_0)\big\|^2
```

Sampling integrates the ODE $dx/dt=v_\theta$ with few Euler steps. Simpler than DDPM, straighter paths → fewer steps; used by
Stable Diffusion 3, Flux, Voicebox, F5-TTS, CosyVoice and others.

**Few-step generation:** progressive distillation (halve steps repeatedly), **consistency models** (map any point on a trajectory
to its origin; 1–4 steps), adversarial distillation (SDXL-Turbo), reflow (straighten flows).

**Diffusion and flows for speech enhancement:** **SGMSE+** defines a forward SDE from clean to noisy speech in the complex STFT
domain and learns the score to reverse it; **StoRM** first predicts with a fast discriminative model, then refines with a few
diffusion steps; flow-matching enhancers reduce steps further. Pros: excellent quality and naturalness at low SNR; cons: many
network evaluations (latency/compute), possible hallucination of phonemes — so for embedded real-time use, discriminative models
(or one-step distilled generative models) still dominate.

**Diffusion language models** (LLaDA, Mercury, Gemini Diffusion): masked/discrete diffusion over tokens, generating many tokens in
parallel — an active area worth knowing exists.

> **Interview angle.** "Could you use diffusion for in-car noise suppression?" — say what it buys (quality at very low SNR),
> what it costs (N forward passes per frame, latency, hallucination risk), and how you'd make it viable (latent/low-res, 1–2 step
> distilled model, or discriminative first pass + light refinement).

## D5 · Self-supervised and multimodal learning

**Contrastive learning** pulls positive pairs together and pushes negatives apart. **InfoNCE** for a batch of N pairs:

```math
\mathcal L=-\frac1N\sum_i\log\frac{\exp(\mathrm{sim}(z_i,z_i^+)/\tau)}{\sum_j\exp(\mathrm{sim}(z_i,z_j)/\tau)}
```

Bigger batches = more negatives; temperature $\tau$ controls hardness. **SimCLR** (augmentations as positives, projection head),
**MoCo** (momentum encoder + queue of negatives), **CLIP** (image–text pairs, symmetric cross-entropy over the similarity
matrix; zero-shot classification by text prompts; CLAP for audio–text).

**Non-contrastive:** BYOL, SimSiam (predictor + stop-gradient avoid collapse without negatives), **DINO/DINOv2** (self-distillation
with centring/sharpening — strong general visual features).

**Masked modelling:** BERT (tokens), **MAE** (mask 75% of image patches, reconstruct pixels, asymmetric encoder/decoder —
efficient), wav2vec 2.0/HuBERT/data2vec (speech). **JEPA** predicts representations of masked parts instead of pixels.

**Multimodal LLMs:** a modality encoder (ViT/MoonViT, Whisper-style audio encoder) + a projector/adapter into the LLM's token
space (LLaVA-style), trained in stages (align projector → instruction tuning). The 2026 trend is **native multimodality**:
Qwen3.5 and Kimi K2.5/K3 train text and vision tokens jointly from pre-training (Kimi K2.5 keeps a constant text/vision ratio and
uses MoonViT-3D with NaViT-style packing of native-resolution images and videos). Omni models (Qwen-Omni family) add audio in and
speech out.

> **Interview angle.** "Why does CLIP need large batches?" — the denominator uses in-batch negatives; more negatives give a
> tighter bound on mutual information and harder negatives. SigLIP's sigmoid loss removes the global softmax and scales better.

## Questions

??* d1 [vae] | Derive the VAE objective and explain the reparameterisation trick.
log p(x) = log ∫ p(x|z)p(z)dz ≥ E_q[log p(x|z)] − KL(q(z|x)||p(z)) by Jensen's inequality (or because log p(x) − ELBO =
KL(q(z|x)||p(z|x)) ≥ 0). The first term rewards reconstruction, the second keeps the posterior close to the prior. Sampling z ~ q is
not differentiable w.r.t. the encoder's parameters, so write z = μ + σ⊙ε with ε ~ N(0, I): the noise is an input and gradients flow
through μ and σ with low variance (unlike the score-function estimator).

?? d1 [vae] | What is posterior collapse and how do you mitigate it?
With a powerful autoregressive decoder, the model can maximise the ELBO by ignoring z: the encoder outputs the prior (KL = 0) and the
decoder models x alone, so latents carry no information. Mitigations: KL annealing (warm up the KL weight), free bits (don't penalise
KL below a threshold per dimension), weakening the decoder, δ-VAE constraints, or discrete latents (VQ-VAE).

??* d1 [vq] | How does a VQ-VAE train through a non-differentiable nearest-neighbour lookup?
The encoder output z_e is replaced by its nearest codebook vector e. In the backward pass, the straight-through estimator copies the
decoder's gradient w.r.t. e directly to z_e. A codebook loss ||sg[z_e] − e||² moves codes toward encoder outputs (or codes are updated
by EMA/k-means), and a commitment loss β||z_e − sg[e]||² keeps the encoder close to its chosen codes. Dead codes are re-initialised
from encoder outputs.

?? d2 [gan, audio] | Why are GAN losses still used in neural vocoders and codecs when diffusion dominates images?
Waveform generation must be fast (real-time, on device) and phase-coherent; adversarial training with multi-period/multi-scale
discriminators produces sharp, realistic waveforms in a single forward pass, while pure regression losses give buzzy, over-smoothed
audio and diffusion needs many steps. HiFi-GAN/BigVGAN-style losses (adversarial + feature matching + mel loss) are therefore standard
in vocoders and codecs like EnCodec and DAC.

??* d3 [diffusion] | Explain the DDPM training objective and why training doesn't simulate the noising chain.
Because Gaussian noising composes, x_t can be sampled in closed form: x_t = √ᾱ_t x₀ + √(1−ᾱ_t) ε. Training samples a random t and ε,
constructs x_t directly, and minimises ||ε − ε_θ(x_t, t)||², a reweighted variational bound equivalent to denoising score matching
(ε_θ ∝ −score). Each training step is a single network call at a random noise level; only sampling runs the chain.

?? d3 [diffusion] | What is classifier-free guidance?
Train one conditional model that sometimes sees a null condition (dropout of the conditioning, ~10–20%). At sampling time combine
predictions: ε̃ = (1+w)·ε(x,c) − w·ε(x,∅). This extrapolates toward the conditional direction, increasing fidelity to the condition and
sample quality at the cost of diversity, without needing a separate classifier. It doubles compute per step unless distilled.

?? d3 [diffusion] | How does DDIM sample faster than DDPM without retraining?
DDIM defines a family of non-Markovian forward processes with the same marginals q(x_t|x₀), so the same trained ε-network applies. Its
reverse update first estimates x̂₀ from x_t and ε_θ, then moves deterministically (η = 0) to x_{t−k} along that estimate, allowing large
jumps over a subsequence of timesteps (e.g. 50 instead of 1000). It's equivalent to an ODE solver for the probability-flow ODE.

??* d4 [flow-matching] | What is flow matching, and why does it allow fewer sampling steps?
Define straight-line paths between noise x₀ and data x₁, x_t = (1−t)x₀ + t·x₁, and train a velocity network to regress the constant
velocity x₁ − x₀ at random t. Sampling integrates dx/dt = v_θ from noise to data with an ODE solver. Training is a simple regression with
no noise schedule, and the learned paths are closer to straight (more so after reflow), so few Euler steps give good samples; it's used in
modern image, TTS and speech-enhancement models.

?? d4 [diffusion, speech] | What are the pros and cons of diffusion-based speech enhancement for real-time systems?
Pros: high perceptual quality and naturalness, better behaviour at very low SNR and on unseen noise, and the ability to generate missing
content. Cons: tens of network evaluations per output (latency and compute far above discriminative models), stochastic outputs, and
risk of hallucinating phonemes or speaker characteristics; streaming is awkward. Mitigations: predictive + few-step refinement (StoRM),
flow matching, distillation to 1–2 steps, smaller latent representations.

??* d5 [contrastive] | Write the InfoNCE/CLIP loss and explain the role of temperature and batch size.
For normalised embeddings, compute the N×N similarity matrix S/τ; CLIP applies cross-entropy over rows (image→text) and columns
(text→image) with the diagonal as the targets, then averages. Temperature τ sharpens or softens the distribution: small τ focuses on hard
negatives but can be unstable (CLIP learns τ). Each sample uses the other N−1 pairs as negatives, so larger batches give more and harder
negatives and better representations; memory banks/queues (MoCo) or sigmoid losses (SigLIP) reduce the dependence on huge batches.

?? d5 [ssl] | How do BYOL/SimSiam avoid collapse without negative pairs?
They use an asymmetric architecture: an online network with an extra predictor head tries to predict the output of a target network on
another augmentation, and gradients are stopped through the target branch (which in BYOL is an EMA of the online network). The predictor
and stop-gradient prevent the trivial constant solution from being a stable fixed point of the dynamics, even though the loss itself would
allow it.

?? d5 [multimodal, chinese-models] | How are multimodal LLMs built, and what does "native multimodal" mean for Qwen3.5 and Kimi K2.5?
Classic approach: a pretrained vision/audio encoder, a projector mapping its features into the LLM embedding space, and staged training
(align the projector, then instruction-tune) on top of a text-only LLM. "Native" multimodal models train on mixed text and image/video
tokens from pre-training onward, so modalities share representations: Qwen3.5 was trained jointly on text, images and video; Kimi K2.5
continues pre-training of K2 on ~15T mixed tokens with a constant vision ratio and a native-resolution MoonViT-3D encoder using NaViT-style
packing. Reported benefits include better cross-modal reasoning and no degradation of text skills.
